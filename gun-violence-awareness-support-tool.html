Pause. You are not alone, and you have a choice.

Having angry or dark thoughts does not make you a bad person. It means you are hurting and something needs to change. What matters is what you do next.

Strong anger and pain rise fast, but they do come down. Hurting someone cannot be undone. Asking for help can be. Here is a simple plan for right now:

1. Step awayLeave the room or the situation. Put distance between you and anything that could hurt someone. Do the 60 second breathing below.
2. Tell one personA parent, relative, friend, teacher, pastor, coach or counsellor. Say: "I am in a bad place and I need help right now."
3. Get supportTalk to a helpline. They listen without judging, and it is free. If you feel you might act on a thought, call emergency services now.

Why reaching out is the stronger choice: it protects other people, it protects your future, and it protects the people who love you. Most people who ask for help are met with care, not punishment.

Talk to someone now

Worried about someone else?

Trust your gut. You do not need to be sure to speak up. Acting early can prevent harm and help someone get support.

Things worth taking seriously:

  • Talking about wanting to hurt others, or about having a weapon to do it
  • Sudden anger, obsession with revenge, or pulling away from everyone
  • Saying goodbye, giving things away, or "they will all be sorry" type comments

What you can do:

  • Stay calm. Listen and let them talk. Do not argue or shame them.
  • Say what you have noticed: "I am worried about you. I care about you."
  • Ask a trusted adult, school staff, faith leader or family member to help.
  • If there is a weapon or a threat, call emergency services right away.

Telling someone is not betrayal. It can be the thing that keeps everyone safe, including the person you are worried about.

For teens: why asking for help beats the alternative

Being a teenager can be really hard. Pressure, bullying, anger and feeling invisible are real. You deserve support, and you are not weak for needing it.

Feelings pass. Actions do not.The worst moment feels like it will last forever, but it does not. Hurting someone stays forever, for them, for you and for both families.
Asking for help is a power move.It takes more courage to say "I am struggling" than to act out of anger. You stay in control of your own life.
You protect your future.Your goals, your friends and your freedom are all still ahead of you. One violent act can end all of it.
Adults usually respond with care.Most people want to help once they know. You do not need the perfect words. You just need to start.
You are not the only one.Many young people carry dark thoughts and anger. Telling someone is how it gets lighter.

Need words? Copy one and send it:

I am not okay and I need to talk. Can you stay with me for a bit?
I have been having really angry thoughts and I need help. Please do not judge me.
Something is going on and I am scared of what I might do. Can you help me get support?

Who you can tell today: a parent or guardian, an older sibling or cousin, a teacher, a school counsellor, a coach, a pastor or imam, or a helpline.

If you are in danger, or think you might hurt someone, call emergency services now. If you know someone is planning to hurt others, tell a trusted adult straight away. That could save lives.
