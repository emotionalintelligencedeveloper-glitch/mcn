Feelings Detective Course

A short course of 10 tricky questions about feelings. Pick any answer. Every choice teaches you something.

How to play

1. Pick an answer. Go with your first gut feeling.
2. Read the clue. You will find out what that answer teaches, and the smartest move.
3. Tap the other answers. Each one has its own lesson. Detectives check every clue.
4. Collect stars. You earn a star when your first pick is the smartest move. Everyone finishes with new ideas.

Why this helps: a note for teachers and parents

Feelings Detective is a free, five to ten minute activity for ages 7 and up. It needs no sign up, no account, and collects no personal information.

Builds emotional vocabularyLearners practise telling a thought from a feeling and naming what they notice.
Safe to get it "wrong"Every answer gets a kind explanation, so children learn without shame or fear of failing.
Teaches the pauseQuestions model stopping, breathing, and choosing a response before reacting.
Grows empathyLearners practise listening, asking instead of guessing, and seeing another point of view.
Supports healthy boundariesShows that being kind and being clear can go together.
Fits real classroomsWorks on phones, tablets, laptops, and shared screens. No downloads needed.

Ideas for using it in class

  • Project it on a screen and let the class vote on each answer before revealing the clue.
  • Pair up learners and ask them to explain why each answer teaches something.
  • Use it as a calm start to the day or a short circle time activity.
  • Ask: "Which clue surprised you most?" and "When did this happen to you or someone you know?"
  • Send the link home so families can play together and talk about it.

Discussion questions

  • What is the difference between a feeling and a thought?
  • What helps you calm down when a feeling is very big?
  • How can we check how someone feels without guessing?

This is a learning and reflection activity. It is not a replacement for professional care. If a child seems to be struggling, please speak to a trusted adult, school counsellor, or health professional.
